Summary: Who I am and what this site is.

I'm Ben Gras, a security researcher working on hardware and systems
security — nearly 30 years across the stack, from microarchitecture
and RTL through firmware to operating systems.

Since 2019 I've been doing offensive security research at Intel on
client platform products, spanning software, firmware, and pre-silicon
RTL. I conceived an AI-first firmware fuzzer and led the team that
built it; it found significant vulnerabilities in highly privileged
firmware and was adopted into a business unit's CI. On the pre-silicon
side I co-developed CellIFT with ETH Zürich (USENIX Security '22) and
IODyne, a differential dataflow-tracking technique (US Patent
12,769,913).

Before Intel I did my PhD at VUSec, the systems security group at VU
Amsterdam, on software-exploitable hardware behavior: TLBleed (USENIX
Security '18), ASLR on the Line (NDSS '17), and more. That work earned
the Pwnie Award for Most Innovative Research (2017) and the IEEE/IFIP
William C. Carter and EuroSys Roger Needham PhD thesis awards (2023),
and took me to stages at Black Hat USA & EU, USENIX Security, NDSS,
and hardwear.io.

Before all that I spent nine years on MINIX 3 in Andrew Tanenbaum's
group — virtual memory, the ARM port, the NetBSD userland integration —
which is the era most of the posts on this blog are from: RTEMS on
BeagleBoards, MINIX releases, JTAG adventures. I'm still a founding
board member of the [RTEMS Foundation](https://www.rtems.org/), which
stewards the real-time OS used in spaceflight.

Away from the keyboard I write and perform a comedy talkshow, box at
competition level, and volunteer at a primary school teaching chess
and helping children with reading.

Find me on [GitHub](https://github.com/bengras) and
[LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/bengras).
